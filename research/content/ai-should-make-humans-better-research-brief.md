# Research Brief: AI Should Make Humans Better, Not Make Humans Obsolete

**Status:** Research complete; ready for article drafting
**Date:** 2 September 2026
**Brand:** Ikhaya Automations

## Strategic context

Owner direction supersedes the previous near-term product-sales stream. Ikhaya Automations should focus on education and audience-building rather than forcing paid digital products. Potential monetisation can come later through advertising, partnerships, customer journeys or occasional products that are genuinely justified by demand.

This article is the current content priority because it expresses a core brand belief: AI should increase human capability rather than remove human judgement.

## Recommended thesis

**AI is most valuable when it extends human capability rather than replacing human judgement.**

The article should not argue that AI is harmless or that jobs will not change. It should argue that good AI use removes friction, spreads capability and gives humans more room for judgement, critical thinking, creativity, context and connection. Human-centred AI is a design choice, not an automatic outcome.

## Evidence base

### 1. Generative AI can disproportionately help less-experienced workers

NBER research by Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li and Lindsey Raymond studied 5,179 customer-support agents. Access to a generative-AI conversational assistant increased productivity by about 14% on average, with substantially larger gains for novice and lower-skilled workers. The evidence is consistent with AI helping disseminate some of the practices associated with stronger workers.

Source: NBER Working Paper 31161, *Generative AI at Work* — https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161

Use: strongest empirical example for the proposition that AI can raise capability rather than merely replace labour.

### 2. AI performance has a 'jagged technological frontier'

Harvard Business School / BCG research involving 758 consultants found strong gains on tasks within GPT-4's capabilities, including faster completion and higher assessed quality. However, performance could deteriorate on tasks outside that frontier. The key implication is not 'delegate everything', but learn which tasks AI can safely support and where human judgement must remain active.

Source: HBS working paper / research on *Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier* — https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700

Use: supports the distinction between intelligent delegation and blind reliance.

### 3. Over-reliance can reduce critical-thinking effort

Microsoft Research surveyed 319 knowledge workers about generative AI and critical thinking. Greater confidence in AI was associated with less reported critical-thinking effort. The work also suggests that human cognitive effort shifts toward verification, integration and oversight when AI is used well.

Source: Microsoft Research, *The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking* — https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/

Use: provides the counterweight to simplistic AI optimism. The danger is not only displacement; it is skill atrophy and uncritical acceptance.

### 4. Human skills remain important as AI use grows

The World Economic Forum's *Future of Jobs Report 2025* identifies technological skills as rising in importance while also highlighting analytical thinking, creative thinking, resilience, curiosity and leadership. Its overall labour-market outlook combines substantial job creation with substantial displacement, making a balanced augmentation argument more credible than claiming AI will not affect jobs.

Source: World Economic Forum, *Future of Jobs Report 2025* — https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/

Use: acknowledge disruption while arguing that the value of human capability changes rather than disappears.

## Core arguments to develop

1. **AI should remove friction, not responsibility.** Use AI for summarising, drafting, organising, searching, comparing and repetitive work. Keep human accountability for decisions and consequences.
2. **AI can make less-experienced people more capable.** Use the NBER customer-support evidence.
3. **AI can make experts more effective, but only inside its strengths.** Use the jagged-frontier evidence.
4. **The danger is not only job replacement. It is skill atrophy.** Use the Microsoft critical-thinking evidence.
5. **The better AI becomes, the more important judgement becomes.** Humans define goals, provide context, challenge assumptions, verify quality and own outcomes.
6. **The goal is better humans with better tools.** Do not make humans compete with AI at machine-like work.

## Useful editorial framing

Bad AI use: **'Do this for me.'**

Better AI use: **'Help me do this better.'**

Best AI use: **'Take the parts that don't need me, challenge the parts that do, and leave the judgement with me.'**

This is Ikhaya editorial framing, not a research finding.

## Recommended article direction

Working title: **AI Should Make Humans Better, Not Make Humans Obsolete**

Alternative: **What If the Point of AI Is to Make Us Better at Being Human?**

Potential anchor line: **The future of work should not be humans competing with AI at being machines. It should be humans using AI to spend more time doing the things machines are bad at.**

This is editorial wording, not a quotation.

## Drafting brief

Target approximately 1,200–1,600 words in UK English. Open with the fear that AI is coming for human jobs, then challenge the assumption that AI success should be measured by how many people it replaces. Move through augmentation evidence, the jagged frontier, critical-thinking risk, genuine labour-market disruption, and a practical model for good human-AI work design. Finish grounded and optimistic rather than utopian.

Tone: intelligent but accessible; practical; calm; plain English; professional without corporate jargon; no hype; no fearmongering; no em dashes; no sales pitch; no generic 'AI revolution' language.
