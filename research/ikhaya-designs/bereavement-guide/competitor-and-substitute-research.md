# Competitor and Substitute Research

**Research date:** 9 September 2026
**Stage:** COMPETITOR / SUBSTITUTE RESEARCH

## Executive finding

The underlying problem is real, but the substitute environment is much stronger than a simple Etsy scan suggests. Free authoritative guidance is extensive, and several UK digital services now provide notification, tracking or personalised task support for free or low cost. This materially weakens a proposition based on "complete checklist" alone.

There is still a plausible paid niche for a low-cost, private, printable/fillable **administration control system** for buyers who prefer an offline or self-managed workspace and do not want to create accounts or upload sensitive estate data to a service. That proposition needs to be tested and positioned narrowly.

## Free authoritative substitutes

### GOV.UK / devolved government

Strengths: authoritative, current procedural guidance, step-by-step journey, Tell Us Once, estate valuation, probate and tax links. Free.

Gap: information is distributed across pages and is primarily guidance, not a personal case-management workspace.

### Marie Curie

Current practical guidance (published June 2026) includes a step-by-step checklist spanning the first practical steps after a death. Free and trusted.

Gap: again, guidance/checklist rather than a persistent administrative case record.

### Cruse and other bereavement charities

Strong emotional-support and signposting substitutes. They reduce any justification for Ikhaya to drift into grief counselling content.

## Digital service substitutes

### Settld

Free bereavement administration service. It can notify multiple companies, track updates, support probate/property tasks and provide a dedicated assistant. It states it works with 1,400+ organisations and funds the free service through partner introductions.

Commercial implication: a paid PDF cannot credibly claim that it uniquely solves multi-company notification or tracking.

### NotifyNOW

Free notification service focused on uploading information once and notifying chosen companies. Again reduces paid value of a simple "who to notify" list.

### Behalf

Offers a free UK-specific personalised checklist across England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, organised by timing. This is a direct substitute for a generic jurisdiction-aware checklist.

### Life Ledger

A direct notification and tracking service for 1,000+ UK companies. Its current personal account proposition advertises unlimited notifications for £19 and family collaboration. This establishes willingness to pay for reducing notification burden, but also creates a high-functionality substitute at a modest price.

### Passage

Offers a free 90-day plan, drafted letters and guides, with paid support for institution-specific actions. This is particularly close to the "clarity first" proposition and raises the bar for a static digital product.

### Lastli

Offers UK-specific IHT/probate/HMRC trackers, notification checklists and templates, with a digital assistant premium offer advertised at £49 lifetime. It explicitly positions against generic checklists.

### Good Order

Employer/insurer benefit providing personalised task lists, death-notice/document/probate guidance and professional connections. Not necessarily directly purchasable by an Etsy consumer, but demonstrates category sophistication.

## Paid printable / downloadable competitors

### Etsy: BenellaHomeOrg, "What to do when someone dies - a checklist to help you (UK)"

Observed price: US$12.42 at research date. One PDF. Five item reviews, all five-star in the surfaced listing, 24 favourites and 166 shop sales. Review language includes "perfect checklist", "nicely organized" and "everything included that you would need". This is modest but genuine evidence that some buyers pay for a simple organised resource despite free information.

### Etsy: northhausUK, UK Funeral Planner / Bereavement Organiser / Probate Tracker

Observed price: US$16.79. Broader organiser positioning, combining funeral, bereavement, probate and executor concepts. Demonstrates that Etsy sellers are already moving beyond single checklists.

### The Bereavement Organiser

Direct-to-consumer PDF at £14.99, 20+ planning pages. Includes first-24-hours checklist, registration, funeral planning, financial accounts, documents, executor/probate planning and digital accounts. This is a close direct competitor to the initial Ikhaya hypothesis.

## Competitive gap analysis

### Weak differentiation

These are not sufficient reasons to build:

- "UK-specific"
- "step-by-step"
- "who to notify"
- "everything in one place"
- "probate tracker"
- "first 30 days"
- "printable checklist"

All are already available.

### More defensible niche

A possible gap remains at the intersection of:

- **offline/private by default**: no account creation or uploading estate data;
- **low cognitive load**: staged action dashboard rather than information-heavy guide;
- **administrative control**: reusable information sheet, contact log, response/follow-up tracker, document/request tracker, outstanding-actions view;
- **authority-linked**: procedural claims kept short with dated links/QR links to official sources;
- **scope discipline**: not pretending to replace notification platforms or probate advice;
- **print + fillable digital use**: useful for a folder shared by family/executors as well as on screen.

This is not proven unique, but it is more credible than another bereavement planner.

## Pricing evidence

Observed direct downloadable products cluster roughly around £10-£17 (after currency differences), while richer digital services range from free to approximately £19 or £49 lifetime in the examples found. A static/fillable Ikhaya product would therefore face strong price anchoring. A preliminary test range around **£9-£15** is more credible than premium-guide pricing unless the product demonstrates unusually strong utility. Final pricing is not authorised at this stage.
