## 2. How to use this organiser

You do not need to complete this organiser from beginning to end. Start with **Your Next Steps**, then use only the pages that are relevant to you.

Use it to:
- see what needs attention now and what can wait;
- keep important information together;
- record organisations you have contacted;
- track documents, responses and follow-ups;
- keep questions for solicitors, banks, HMRC or other professionals in one place.

**Important:** This organiser provides practical organisation and signposting only. It is not legal, tax, probate or financial advice. Procedures can change. Check the official sources on the final pages before acting on procedural information.

**Privacy:** A completed copy may contain sensitive personal and financial information. Store it securely. Do not record passwords, PINs or security answers in this organiser.

## 3. Your Next Steps — Now / Soon / Later

### NOW — focus only on what needs immediate attention
- Follow the appropriate official process to have the death medically certified and registered.
- Check whether the death has been referred to a coroner.
- Find out whether the person left a will and where it is held.
- Identify who is taking responsibility for the immediate administration.
- Arrange the funeral or other arrangements when appropriate.
- Keep death certificates and important documents together.
- Record urgent property, pet, dependent or security matters.

### SOON — once the immediate steps are under control
- Use Tell Us Once if available to you.
- Identify private organisations that still need to be contacted.
- Begin listing bank accounts, pensions, investments, property, debts, bills and subscriptions.
- Record every contact, reference number, document request and promised response.
- Identify the executor(s) named in the will, or check the official intestacy guidance if there is no will.

### LATER — estate administration and closure
- Work through outstanding organisations and follow-ups.
- Gather information needed to value the estate.
- Check whether probate or letters of administration may be required using current GOV.UK guidance.
- Keep a record of professional advice and decisions.
- Review recurring payments, digital accounts, memberships and household arrangements.
- Keep an outstanding-actions list until administration is complete.

**Not everything applies to every death or estate. If you are unsure, use the official guidance rather than guessing.**

## 4. When the usual route does not apply

### If a coroner is involved
The registration process and timing can differ. Follow the coroner/registrar's instructions and current GOV.UK guidance rather than the standard checklist in this organiser.

### If the person died abroad
There are additional country-specific and UK requirements. Use GOV.UK's current guidance for deaths abroad before relying on the standard route.

### If circumstances are complex
Seek appropriate professional advice for complex estates, disputes, trusts, overseas assets, uncertain entitlement, tax questions or other circumstances outside straightforward administration.

# Internal Build Critique

## What passes

1. **Differentiation:** the strongest pages are the contact/follow-up log, document tracker, information-request tracker and outstanding-actions review. Together they create a working control system rather than another reading-heavy checklist.
2. **Cognitive load:** Now/Soon/Later staging and the outstanding-actions page give the user an obvious re-entry point when administration becomes overwhelming.
3. **Scope discipline:** funeral planning, grief counselling and detailed legal/tax instruction are deliberately excluded.
4. **Privacy:** password capture is explicitly prohibited and sensitive-data warnings are repeated where useful.
5. **Maintenance:** volatile monetary thresholds, probate fees and tax figures are not embedded.

## Revision points before design

- Verify every procedural sentence and every official URL against current authoritative sources immediately before final production.
- The Now/Soon/Later page must not visually imply every action is mandatory or strictly chronological.
- In design, split the wide contact log intelligently across landscape or two complementary pages if A4 portrait becomes cramped. Do not shrink text to force the table onto a page.
- Make the control pages visually dominant in product previews; they are the paid-value layer.
- Keep botanical decoration restrained and outside working/table areas.
- Do not market the product as "everything you need" or imply it replaces Tell Us Once, probate guidance or professional advice.
