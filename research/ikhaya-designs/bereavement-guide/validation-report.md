# Bereavement Administration Product Validation Report

**Research date:** 9 September 2026
**Decision gate:** OWNER APPROVAL REQUIRED
**Recommendation:** **BUILD, WITH A NARROWED V1**

## Executive conclusion

The customer problem is strongly validated. UK research shows substantial administrative difficulty during bereavement, and the current official process requires users to coordinate multiple stages, organisations, documents and follow-ups while grieving.

However, the original broad idea needs narrowing. The market now contains excellent free authoritative guidance and increasingly capable free/low-cost digital notification and bereavement-admin services. Ikhaya should **not** build another "complete guide to what to do when someone dies" or generic bereavement planner.

The credible paid opportunity is a **calm, low-cognitive-load bereavement administration control system** for people who want a printable/fillable workspace to organise their own case without relying on an app or uploading sensitive estate information.

## Validation gate answers

### 1. Is there sufficient evidence of a meaningful customer problem?

**Yes, strong evidence.**

A peer-reviewed 2024 analysis of UK Commission on Bereavement survey responses reports that 61% of adults bereaved in the previous five years had difficulty with at least one practical or administrative task. The qualitative findings identify administrative burden, difficult paperwork, repeated disclosure, chasing organisations and organisational failures as recurring problems.

### 2. Is the problem inadequately solved by free authoritative resources alone?

**Partly.**

Free resources solve the information problem very well. GOV.UK, devolved-government sources and Marie Curie provide strong guidance and checklists. They do not fully solve personal case management: recording what applies, who has been contacted, documents sent, promises made, follow-up dates, reference numbers and outstanding actions.

The paid value must therefore be organisation/control, not access to procedural information.

### 3. Is there evidence that customers buy or actively seek organisational help?

**Yes, but not enough to justify a large build.**

Etsy has paid UK checklist/organiser products with purchases/reviews/favourites, and direct-to-consumer organisers sell around the mid-teens. Life Ledger charges for notification/tracking functionality and Lastli advertises a higher-priced digital assistant. This establishes willingness to pay for reduced administrative burden, while also demonstrating strong substitutes.

### 4. What is the specific paid value beyond repackaging free information?

A persistent control system that helps the user:

- see urgent, next and later actions;
- collect core details/documents once;
- identify which private organisations still need contact;
- record each contact, requirement and outcome;
- record documents/certificates sent;
- capture reference numbers and date-of-death balances;
- schedule and track follow-ups;
- see unresolved actions at a glance;
- optionally coordinate responsibility between family/executors;
- jump to dated authoritative sources when a rule/procedure needs checking.

### 5. Who is the primary buyer/user?

**Primary:** an adult family member, executor or administrator handling a relatively straightforward estate and practical notifications after an adult death.

The product should not assume the buyer is legally the executor from page one. It should work for the person coordinating practical administration and clearly distinguish estate/executor-only tasks.

### 6. What jurisdiction should v1 cover?

**Recommendation: England and Wales v1.**

Reason: the largest addressable UK jurisdiction can be covered accurately with one coherent process. Scotland and Northern Ireland have material differences in registration, terminology and estate/probate procedure. Whole-UK coverage would increase complexity, QA burden and maintenance before demand is proven.

The product can state clearly that v1 is for England and Wales. Scotland and Northern Ireland can become later variants if sales justify them.

### 7. What exact format best solves the problem?

**Printable + fillable PDF administration system, not an ebook.**

Recommended v1 modules:

1. **Start here: what needs attention now** - a one-page staged map, with clear signposts for coroner/death-abroad exceptions.
2. **Core information sheet** - deceased details, key contacts, will/executor information, certificate/reference information.
3. **Documents and copies control** - what exists, where held, copies requested/sent/returned.
4. **First actions dashboard** - registration, funeral, Tell Us Once, immediate property/security, relevant benefits/signposting.
5. **Who still needs telling** - category-based private-organisation inventory, explicitly complementary to Tell Us Once.
6. **Contact and follow-up log** - organisation, date, channel, contact, requirement, documents, reference, next action, due/follow-up, status.
7. **Assets / debts / account discovery worksheet** - organisational capture only, linked to official estate-valuation guidance.
8. **Estate administration progress map** - will/intestacy, valuation/IHT decision point, probate decision point, debts/taxes, distribution, with official-source links rather than legal instruction.
9. **Outstanding actions view** - a short review page to regain control after interruptions.
10. **Useful official links and support** - dated authoritative sources and bereavement-support signposting.

Avoid a large grief-journaling section, funeral aesthetic filler, legal templates presented as universal, or pages that exist only to inflate page count.

### 8. What is the evidence-based price range?

**Provisional test range: £9-£15.**

Comparable downloadable organisers are around the low-to-mid teens, while functional digital services can be free or inexpensive. This creates a hard ceiling for a static product. Price should be finalised only after the product's actual depth and Etsy positioning are complete.

### 9. What are the strongest Etsy/Pinterest discovery routes?

Etsy: functional/buyer language around what to do after a death, bereavement admin/checklist, executor checklist, who to notify, probate/estate organisation and document tracking. Exact tags should be validated against current Etsy search data at listing stage.

Pinterest: problem/solution recognition rather than generic product covers. Strongest conceptual route is "I have been given guidance, but how do I keep track of all of this?" Show the real system, especially dashboards, contact tracking and outstanding actions.

### 10. What factual/legal maintenance burden would the product create?

**Moderate and manageable if scope is disciplined.**

High-risk procedural details should live behind official links rather than be duplicated extensively. Date-stamp the guide and review at least annually, plus when major death-registration, Tell Us Once, probate or IHT processes change. Avoid hard-coding volatile fees/thresholds unless necessary.

### 11. What material risks remain?

- emotional sensitivity and reputational risk if marketing becomes manipulative;
- users mistaking organisational guidance for legal/probate/tax advice;
- process changes making static wording stale;
- competitive pressure from free notification/admin platforms;
- weak conversion if the product looks like a generic printable checklist;
- privacy concerns if a fillable file contains sensitive estate/account information. The product should include practical storage/privacy guidance and should not encourage recording passwords.

### 12. Recommendation

**BUILD, WITH A NARROWED V1**, subject to Michaela's approval of the decision gate.

Do not build a whole-UK bereavement ebook. Build an England-and-Wales administration control system whose paid value is cognitive relief, organisation and follow-through.

## Critical review

### Sceptical customer view

"Why would I pay when GOV.UK, Marie Curie, Behalf and notification services are free?"

The product only earns the purchase if the listing visibly demonstrates the working pages: a staged dashboard, contact/follow-up log, document control and outstanding-actions view. A cover image plus claims about being comprehensive will fail this test.

### Commercial reviewer view

The category is commercially credible but not a likely high-ticket product. It fits Ikhaya Designs GB exceptionally well and can produce durable Pinterest/search content, but build effort must remain proportionate. The first version should be tightly scoped and reusable, not a 70-page flagship built before sales evidence.

### Quality/risk reviewer view

England-and-Wales-only v1 materially improves accuracy. The system should distinguish factual instructions, organisational prompts and "check official guidance" decision points visually. It should never promise exhaustiveness for every estate.

## Proposed build specification if approved

**Working product type:** Bereavement Administration Organiser for England & Wales

**Transformation:** from scattered paperwork and mental load to one calm view of what needs doing, what has been done and what still needs follow-up.

**Target length:** approximately 18-28 genuinely useful pages, depending on repeated log sheets. Utility matters more than page count.

**Delivery:** A4 printable PDF plus fillable PDF if technically robust. Consider a US Letter version only if evidence shows non-UK demand, but UK procedural content remains England/Wales specific.

**Visual direction:** Ikhaya Designs GB greens/neutrals, restrained botanical detail, high whitespace, strong functional hierarchy. Avoid sombre funeral clichés and decorative clutter.

**Must include:** clear scope/disclaimer, date/version, official source links, privacy/storage note, no-password warning, support signposting.

**Must not include:** personalised legal/tax advice, unsupported claims, manipulative grief sales language, generic filler trackers, or procedural claims copied from non-authoritative competitors.
