# UK What To Do When Someone Dies Guide

**Brand:** Ikhaya Designs GB
**Stage:** RESEARCH / VALIDATION
**Authorised:** 8 September 2026
**Build status:** NOT AUTHORISED until validation gate passes

## Purpose

Research and validate a UK-specific practical product that helps a recently bereaved person understand, organise and track the administrative tasks that follow a death.

The aim is not to replace GOV.UK, solicitors, probate professionals, funeral directors, financial advisers or bereavement services. The potential paid value is reducing cognitive load by turning fragmented and sometimes conditional information into a calm, usable action system with appropriate links back to authoritative sources.

## Working proposition

A calm, plain-English bereavement administration system for people asking: **What do I actually need to do now?**

It may eventually include staged checklists, information/document collection, organisations-to-notify tracking, contact/call logs, funeral administration, accounts and subscriptions, estate/probate administration and progress tracking. This is a hypothesis, not an approved specification.

Do not design the finished PDF, create an Etsy listing, set a final price or publish anything until the validation gate below passes.

## Research questions

### 1. UK process and factual map

Build an accurate journey map using current authoritative sources. Cover at minimum:

- immediate steps after a death;
- medical examiner / certification process where applicable;
- registering a death and applicable deadlines;
- coroner/inquest variants;
- death abroad variants;
- Tell Us Once, eligibility, information required, what it covers and what remains outside it;
- organisations outside government that commonly need notification;
- funeral arrangements and possible financial support;
- bereavement benefits and effects on the survivor's benefits/tax/pension where relevant;
- wills, executors and administrators;
- estate valuation and Inheritance Tax decision points;
- probate / letters of administration and when professional advice may be appropriate;
- debts, taxes, creditor notices, estate administration and distribution;
- property, vehicles, digital accounts, utilities, insurance, banking, pensions, memberships and subscriptions;
- bereavement support and safeguarding/signposting.

Separate England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland wherever the process or terminology materially differs. Never imply one UK-wide rule where there are jurisdictional differences.

Initial authoritative baseline: GOV.UK's current after-a-death journey confirms a sequence covering registration, government notification, funeral arrangements, bereavement benefits/support, estate valuation and Inheritance Tax, probate and estate administration. Tell Us Once applies in England, Scotland and Wales but not Northern Ireland and does not remove the need to contact many private organisations such as banks, mortgage providers, insurers, utilities, landlords and many pension schemes.

### 2. User problem research

Investigate what bereaved people actually find difficult about the administrative process. Look for recurring themes such as:

- not knowing what to do first;
- uncertainty about what is urgent versus what can wait;
- fragmented guidance across organisations;
- repeatedly supplying the same information;
- losing track of who has been contacted and what they said;
- identifying accounts, subscriptions and organisations;
- understanding probate/estate terminology;
- managing documents and reference numbers;
- coordinating tasks between family members/executors;
- cognitive overload while grieving;
- fear of missing something important.

Use credible qualitative evidence and distinguish anecdotes from broad evidence. Do not manufacture customer pain points.

### 3. Competitor and substitute research

Research:

- Etsy bereavement/death administration planners, checklists and probate organisers;
- UK-specific paid guides and organisers;
- free GOV.UK and devolved-government guidance;
- Citizens Advice and major bereavement charities;
- funeral-director checklists;
- bank/bereavement-service resources;
- probate/solicitor resources;
- relevant digital apps or services.

For competitors record product type, geography, price where visible, scope, presentation, reviews/customer language where legitimately available, strengths, weaknesses and apparent gaps.

The existence of free authoritative guidance is a central substitute and must be treated seriously. Determine why someone would pay for the Ikhaya product rather than simply use GOV.UK.

### 4. Search and discoverability research

Investigate customer language and intent across Etsy, Pinterest and web search. Test themes around phrases such as:

- what to do when someone dies UK;
- what to do after a death checklist;
- bereavement checklist UK;
- executor checklist UK;
- probate organiser / probate checklist;
- who to notify when someone dies;
- death administration checklist;
- bereavement planner / organiser.

Do not assume these are good keywords merely because they sound plausible. Record evidence, intent and platform fit.

### 5. Commercial assessment

Assess:

- evidence of a real problem;
- evidence of purchase intent / willingness to pay;
- discoverability route;
- competition and quality of substitutes;
- differentiation opportunity;
- appropriate product format;
- likely price range based on evidence;
- build effort and maintenance burden;
- requirement for periodic legal/process review;
- platform fit with Etsy and Pinterest;
- brand fit with Ikhaya Designs GB;
- legal, factual, reputational and emotional-sensitivity risks.

## Product principles to test

The research should specifically test whether the strongest proposition is an **administration system rather than an ebook**.

Potential value proposition:

> Official guidance tells you what the rules are. Ikhaya helps you see what you need to do, what can wait, what information you need, who you have contacted and what remains unfinished.

This wording is a research hypothesis only and is not approved customer-facing copy.

The finished product, if authorised, should be calm rather than clinical, supportive without becoming grief counselling, practical without pretending to give personalised legal/financial advice, and designed for reduced cognitive capacity during bereavement.

## Safety and accuracy rules

- Prefer primary/authoritative sources for procedural claims.
- Date-stamp research and retain source links.
- Clearly identify jurisdiction-specific rules.
- Do not provide personalised legal, tax, probate or financial advice.
- Flag situations where professional advice may be appropriate rather than attempting to solve complex estates.
- Avoid guarantees that a checklist is exhaustive for every circumstance.
- Do not use manipulative grief-based sales language.
- Do not imply endorsement by government, charities or professional bodies.
- Treat suicide, child death and other particularly sensitive circumstances carefully; do not casually incorporate them into generic marketing.
- No em dashes in customer-facing Ikhaya copy.

## Validation gate

Before BUILD can begin, produce a written validation report that answers:

1. Is there sufficient evidence of a meaningful customer problem?
2. Is the problem inadequately solved by free authoritative resources alone?
3. Is there evidence that customers buy or actively seek organisational help for this problem?
4. What is the specific paid value beyond repackaging free information?
5. Who is the primary buyer/user?
6. What jurisdiction should v1 cover: England and Wales only, Great Britain, or the whole UK with jurisdiction variants?
7. What exact format best solves the problem?
8. What is the evidence-based price range?
9. What are the strongest Etsy/Pinterest discovery routes?
10. What factual/legal maintenance burden would the product create?
11. What material risks remain?
12. Recommendation: BUILD, MODIFY/RESEARCH FURTHER, or DO NOT BUILD.

A BUILD recommendation must include a proposed product specification and evidence trail. A weak commercial case should be abandoned or reframed rather than built because the concept is emotionally compelling.
