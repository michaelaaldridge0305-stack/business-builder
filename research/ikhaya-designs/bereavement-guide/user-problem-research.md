# User Problem Research: Bereavement Administration

**Research date:** 9 September 2026
**Stage:** USER PROBLEM RESEARCH

## Finding

There is strong evidence that the problem is not merely lack of information. The recurring problem is having to understand, sequence, remember, document and chase a large number of administrative interactions while grieving.

A 2024 peer-reviewed secondary analysis of UK Commission on Bereavement survey data analysed verbatim responses from 1,133 UK adults bereaved within the previous five years. It reports that 61% of bereaved adults had difficulties with at least one practical or administrative task. Three themes were identified: needs outside business as usual, administrative burden while grieving, and the impact of organisational failures.

Reported difficulties included resistance when making initial contact, insensitive interactions, unrealistic timelines, difficulty understanding/completing paperwork, repeatedly giving painful information, chasing organisations, administrative errors and uncertainty about coordinating separate organisations.

This is unusually direct evidence for Ikhaya's proposed problem space.

## Recurring jobs-to-be-done

The evidence supports these user needs:

- know what to do first and what can wait;
- see the whole administrative workload without holding it mentally;
- know which organisations still need direct notification after government notification;
- collect core information and documents once;
- track who has been contacted and what happened;
- track promised responses and follow-up dates;
- coordinate tasks across family members/executors;
- separate simple administrative actions from decisions requiring professional advice;
- retain reference numbers and date-of-death balances in one place;
- regain confidence that important tasks have not silently disappeared.

## Cognitive-load design requirement

The product should assume reduced concentration and emotional capacity. It should not require the buyer to read a dense guide before becoming useful. The first interaction should answer three questions quickly:

1. What genuinely needs attention now?
2. What information should I gather?
3. Where can I record what I have already done?

The strongest potential transformation is therefore not "learn about bereavement administration". It is "stop trying to hold bereavement administration in your head".

## What not to build

Research argues against:

- a generic grief journal;
- a long ebook that paraphrases government guidance;
- an exhaustive legal/probate manual;
- a decorative checklist with no status/follow-up system;
- marketing that exploits fear of making a mistake;
- a product that assumes all users are executors or all estates require probate.

## Evidence strength and limitations

**Strong:** UK-specific peer-reviewed qualitative evidence from a large national consultation; current government workflows show multiple organisations, stages and repeated information requirements.

**Moderate:** Customer reviews on paid checklist products show appreciation for organisation/ease, but review volumes are small and cannot establish market size alone.

**Unknown:** How much Etsy buyers specifically will pay for a more sophisticated administration system versus using free digital services. This is a commercial question addressed in competitor/substitute research.
