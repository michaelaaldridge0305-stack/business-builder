## Bereavement resource: active product-development exception

The bereavement product is personally important to Michaela and is explicitly reactivated despite the general preference to sell existing products before creating more. It must not displace time-sensitive Christmas distribution.

### Geographic scope
Michaela's latest direction is **England-specific**. Reconcile any earlier England & Wales scope before final build and ensure procedural claims are sourced from authoritative current government/official sources.

### Product architecture
The product should deliberately have two substantial parts rather than treating emotional support as an appendix.

**Part One: What you need to do**
Research and explain the practical sequence calmly, including registering a death, certificates/documentation, Tell Us Once, funeral-related administration, notifying organisations, estates, probate, finances/accounts and other necessary responsibilities. Clearly distinguish what is mandatory, optional, conditional and time-sensitive.

**Part Two: Looking after the person left behind**
Acknowledge that the administration may slow down while grief remains. Include carefully researched support for general bereavement, loss of a child, suicide bereavement/suicide prevention, Armed Forces-related bereavement where relevant, and people whose identity/routine centred on caring for someone for a long period and who may struggle after that caring role ends. Include trustworthy contact information for relevant organisations.

Also explore gentle non-clinical support such as meditation, grounding/reflection and self-care through grief. These must not be presented as substitutes for professional mental-health support.

### Grief, philosophy and remembrance section
Avoid generic Pinterest-style grief quotes. Research thoughtful perspectives from varied sources, potentially including contemplative/Buddhist or related traditions, *How to Live When a Loved One Dies*, the remembered passage from *The Time Traveler's Wife*, and meaningful ideas about remembrance from Pixar's *Coco*.

Do not invent or misattribute quotations. Verify wording/provenance. Because books and films are copyrighted, use only appropriately brief quotations in a commercial product or express underlying ideas in original Ikhaya wording where appropriate.

Core owner idea to preserve in the product: **we don't talk about grief enough**. The reader should understand that grief has no prescribed timetable and their experience need not resemble someone else's.
