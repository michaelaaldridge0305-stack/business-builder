## First complete AI draft

### Executive summary

September operating profit was £27k, £42k below budget and £46k below last year. Revenue was £120k above budget, although this included a £210k project originally expected in October, so the favourable variance reflects timing rather than additional full-year revenue. Gross margin was below budget, while installation payroll and subcontractor costs were both higher than planned. Receivables also require attention, with a £240k customer balance overdue.

### Priority commentary

1. **Revenue**

   September revenue was £1.42m, £120k above budget and £165k above last year. This included a £210k project brought forward from October. The project remains in the latest full-year forecast, so the September benefit represents timing rather than additional revenue. The October forecast should be rephased accordingly.

2. **Gross profit and margin**

   Gross profit was £398k, £8k above budget. However, gross margin was 28.0% compared with 30.0% budget and last year. The accelerated project required additional subcontract installation support and may have affected the monthly margin, but its separate profitability has not yet been calculated. The project margin and the remaining movement should therefore be analysed before attributing the decline to a specific cause.

3. **Installation costs and operating profit**

   Installation payroll was £29k above budget in September and £116k above budget year to date. Approved overtime associated with three projects explains £18k of the monthly variance, leaving £11k requiring further analysis.

   Subcontractor costs were £24k above budget for the month and £59k above budget year to date. September included £31k connected with the accelerated project, but the budget treatment and remaining movements have not been analysed. Operating profit was consequently £42k below budget despite revenue being above budget.

4. **Receivables**

   Closing receivables were £1.86m, £340k above budget, while debtor days increased to 49 compared with 41 budgeted and 40 last year. A £240k customer balance became overdue during September, of which £35k is disputed. The remaining balance has not been disputed, but no payment date has been confirmed. This represents a collection and credit risk rather than evidence of a confirmed bad debt.

5. **Year-to-date position**

   Year-to-date revenue was £130k above budget, but gross profit was £91k below budget and operating profit was £259k below budget. Gross margin was 28.7% compared with 30.0% budget. Installation payroll and subcontractor costs were respectively £116k and £59k above budget, partly offset by other operating costs being £7k below budget.

### Questions and actions before sign-off

- Rephase the £210k accelerated project from October into September without changing the full-year revenue forecast unless further evidence supports a revision.
- Calculate the project's separate gross margin and assess the remaining overall margin variance.
- Complete the monthly and year-to-date installation payroll analysis, including the £11k monthly variance not explained by approved overtime.
- Reconcile the subcontractor variance and confirm how the accelerated project was reflected in the budget.
- Obtain a payment date for the undisputed portion of the overdue balance and resolve the £35k customer dispute.
- Review the wider movement in receivables and debtor days.

The Finance Manager remains responsible for reviewing and approving the final commentary.

## Participant corrections

No factual, numerical or analytical correction was requested.

One house-style correction was requested: wording such as "has not yet been calculated" or "has not been analysed" could imply laziness or sloppy work. The participant wanted the commentary to distinguish unavailable information from poor performance without inventing a reason for the analysis being incomplete.

The revision therefore used wording such as "the information available for this commentary does not explain..." and "separate project profitability was not available for this commentary."

## Writing and analytical critique

Strengths:

- The revenue timing movement was not presented as additional full-year revenue.
- The margin decline was not attributed to the accelerated project without project-level evidence.
- The £31k project subcontractor cost was not incorrectly treated as a complete explanation of the £24k adverse variance.
- Only £35k of the overdue balance was described as disputed.
- The overdue balance was treated as a collection and credit risk, not a confirmed bad debt.
- Monthly and year-to-date positions were kept separate.
- Actions were specific and proportionate.

Improvement identified by the participant:

- Phrases such as "has not yet been calculated" can sound as though Finance failed to complete its work. The corrected version appropriately states what information was available for the commentary without inventing a reason for the gap.

Further evaluator caution:

- Saying the £18k overtime "explains" £18k of the payroll variance is reasonable for this fictional test and matches the intended analysis, but in a real pack the budget treatment of overtime should be confirmed before treating the whole amount as a variance explanation.
- The final output is accurate but still slightly longer than some MD packs would require. This is a configurable house-style issue, not a reliability defect.
