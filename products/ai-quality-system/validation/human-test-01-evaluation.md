# Human Transfer Test 01 - Product Evaluation

**Date:** 23 August 2026  
**Evaluator:** Ikhaya Digital Product Manager  
**Decision:** MODIFY AND ADVANCE TO A NARROW BETA TEST

## Executive conclusion

Human Transfer Test 01 supports the proposition that finance-specific AI quality controls can produce professional management commentary quickly and with low correction burden.

It does not validate the full three-workflow toolkit. It is one test with one participant, and the participant is also the business owner and a qualified finance professional. The evidence supports progressing the Numbers to Management Narrative workflow, not building the whole original proposition.

## Evidence

- First complete draft produced in 27.59 seconds.
- The participant required two presentation corrections and no numerical or analytical correction.
- Ratings were 4/5 for confidence and 5/5 for ease, helpfulness and repeat-use intent.
- The participant reported that the method was "much, much quicker".
- Nothing was considered unnecessary or cumbersome.
- Indicative willingness to pay was £20 for a polished reusable toolkit.
- Review and total time are invalid because the timer continued during an interruption. They must not be used as performance evidence.

## Independent technical review

### Numerical fidelity

The first draft represented the source data correctly:

- revenue: £980k, £70k below budget and £55k above last year;
- operating profit: £25k, £36k below budget and £17k below last year;
- gross profit: £310k, £16k below budget;
- payroll: £6k adverse for August and £36k adverse year to date;
- marketing: £17k adverse for August and £27k adverse year to date;
- inventory: £1.18m, £250k above budget;
- inventory days: 78 compared with 61 budgeted.

The participant initially referred to 68 inventory days during correction. The source value of 61 was correctly retained.

### Analytical validity

No material unsupported causal claim was found.

The commentary correctly:

- treated the delayed £92k order as timing rather than lost demand;
- avoided treating the supplier rebate as proof of permanent margin improvement;
- treated the new sales manager as a partial explanation only;
- separated the £4k exhibition overspend from the wider £17k monthly marketing variance;
- treated slow-moving stock as a risk indicator rather than a confirmed impairment;
- identified that the approved £160k stock build did not explain the full £250k inventory variance.

### Residual weaknesses

1. The draft used "percentage points", which is technically precise but did not match the participant's normal professional voice.
2. The first inventory-days comparison omitted a repeated unit after the budget value.
3. The corrected inventory sentence, "78 days compared with 61 days budgeted", is accurate but awkward.
4. The commentary repeated several figures between the executive summary and priority points. This is acceptable, but a more concise version would feel more like a polished management pack.
5. The September action should distinguish forecast phasing from full-year outlook. The order moved between months and was not stated to change full-year revenue.
6. Supplier-rebate wording should prompt confirmation of accounting treatment and phasing without implying that recognition is incorrect.

No critical failure was found.

## Correction burden

- Material factual or analytical corrections: 0
- Presentation or house-style corrections: 2
- Discard and rebuild required: No
- Traceback friction: Low
- Timed correction effort: Not valid because the review was interrupted

## What the test validates

Supported:

- the target user can apply concise generic finance checks to a new scenario;
- the checks can produce a strong first draft without a scenario-specific answer key;
- the result can materially reduce typing and drafting effort;
- the workflow has repeat-use appeal;
- a price around £20 is plausible enough to test.

Not yet supported:

- demand from independent customers;
- willingness to pay demonstrated by a purchase or pre-sale;
- use by less experienced finance professionals;
- performance across different AI products and user prompting styles;
- the other two proposed workflows;
- the full six-stage framework as the source of value rather than the job-specific checks and worked example.

## Product decision

**MODIFY AND ADVANCE.**

Progress only the Numbers to Management Narrative workflow as a narrow beta product. Do not build the full three-workflow toolkit yet.

The beta should focus on the demonstrated paid value:

- a reusable management-commentary procedure;
- concise job-specific checks;
- a worked good example;
- plausible-looking failure examples;
- a final review checklist;
- an editable house-style section;
- clear data-handling and human-sign-off boundaries.

Working price hypothesis: **£20**. This is a test price, not a validated permanent price.

## Next evidence gate

Before public build or launch, complete Human Transfer Test 02 and then obtain at least two independent target-user tests. At least one independent participant should not be the product owner.

Advance to beta build only if:

- no critical failure occurs;
- factual or analytical correction burden remains low;
- the participant reports a net time saving;
- the method can be applied without hidden guidance;
- the output is judged professionally usable after proportionate review.

A second successful owner test improves repeatability evidence but cannot replace independent customer validation.
