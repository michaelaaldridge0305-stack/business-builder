# Management Commentary Quality Rules v1.0

**Status:** Active internal build rules  
**Source:** AI benchmark work and Human Transfer Test 01

## Purpose

These rules govern the Numbers to Management Narrative workflow. They are quality controls, not permission for AI to establish the accounting truth. Source figures and accounting judgements remain the responsibility of the finance professional.

## Required controls

Before finalising commentary, the AI must:

1. reproduce figures, percentages, periods and favourable/adverse directions exactly;
2. make the comparator explicit whenever ambiguity is possible;
3. distinguish observed results from explanations and hypotheses;
4. state a cause no more strongly than the evidence supports;
5. show when a known factor explains only part of a variance;
6. distinguish timing or phasing from a change in underlying or full-year performance;
7. avoid escalating a risk indicator into a confirmed loss, impairment, bad debt or operational failure;
8. avoid inventing causes, trends, business facts or missing numbers;
9. prioritise matters that affect a management decision or require action;
10. identify the minimum useful follow-up for material unknowns;
11. keep clear supported conclusions visible rather than burying them in caveats;
12. state that final judgement and sign-off remain with the finance professional.

## Numerical and unit rules

- Include the unit on every standalone metric where omission could create ambiguity.
- Prefer natural comparisons such as "31.6% compared with a budget of 31.0%" when this matches the user's house style.
- Do not write a change as "0.6%" if the intended meaning is a difference between two percentages. Either show both percentages or use "percentage points".
- Do not silently correct a user's spoken figure. Check it against the validated source and retain the source value.
- Do not mix monthly and year-to-date explanations.
- Do not claim that a monthly timing movement changes the full-year outlook without evidence.

## British professional style

- Use concise British English suitable for a management accounts pack.
- Prefer "adverse to budget" or "above/below budget" where helpful, but do not make the prose sound formulaic.
- Avoid em dashes.
- Avoid generic AI phrases, unnecessary scene-setting and repeated conclusions.
- Use confident language for established facts and explicit qualification for uncertainty.
- Prefer direct actions with an owner or purpose where the source supports one.
- Treat house-style preferences separately from technical errors.

## Required output

1. Short executive summary.
2. Three to five priority commentary points.
3. Questions or actions before sign-off.
4. Human sign-off reminder.

## Final verification checklist

- [ ] All quoted figures match the validated source.
- [ ] All units and comparators are clear.
- [ ] No known factor is presented as explaining more than it does.
- [ ] Timing is not presented as underlying growth or decline.
- [ ] No risk indicator is presented as a confirmed outcome.
- [ ] No unsupported cause or trend has been added.
- [ ] Monthly and cumulative positions are not conflated.
- [ ] Actions address the remaining unknowns.
- [ ] The executive summary is not merely a duplicate of the detail.
- [ ] Language matches the user's professional house style.
- [ ] Final judgement remains with the finance professional.
