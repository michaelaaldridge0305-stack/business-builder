# Christmas Gift Organiser: live Etsy competitive pricing audit

**Date:** 20 September 2026
**Market:** Etsy UK buyer view, GBP
**Workstream:** Existing-product conversion, not product rebuilding

## Owner direction applied

Michaela asked that pricing be checked in the actual Etsy buyer environment rather than inferred from generic backend/web research. Current owner-set base price is £1.99, with a recently observed UK buyer-visible price of approximately £2.39. The commercial aim at the shop's current early-sales stage is to favour conversion and social proof rather than maximise margin per sale.

## Live search benchmark

Search used: `Christmas gift planner spreadsheet Google Sheets gift tracker budget`.

Twenty relevant current Etsy results were reviewed. Buyer-visible prices observed:

£1.00, £0.93, £2.53, £2.42, £4.68, £1.58, £4.85, £8.00, £1.96, £9.12, £1.61, £4.68, £2.40, £6.69, £8.89, £3.28, £1.00, £3.74, £5.01, £13.10.

**Median observed price:** £3.51.

**Observed market structure:**
- Entry-price cluster: roughly £0.93 to £2.53, often supported by 30% to 60% sale pricing.
- Mid-range: roughly £3.28 to £5.01.
- Premium: roughly £6.69 to £13.10, generally requiring visibly broader scope, stronger proof, or both.

Several established sellers with substantial review counts are competing at £0.93 to £1.61 sale prices. This means price alone cannot create a durable advantage, but a high price would add friction for a low-review shop.

## Competitive merchandising observations

The strongest listings repeatedly make the product tangible rather than relying on decorative Christmas imagery. Common conversion devices include:

- a real spreadsheet/dashboard shown immediately;
- explicit gift-budget and progress outcomes;
- concrete feature counts or tab counts;
- automatic calculations/dashboard claims;
- clear Google Sheets/Excel compatibility;
- buyer reassurance about ease of use;
- a visible system rather than a generic festive printable;
- sale pricing as a conversion device.

Competitors span very different aesthetics, but the commercial pattern is consistent: buyers are shown what the tool does and what they receive.

## Recommendation

**KEEP the current £1.99 base-price strategy for now. Do not raise it.**

Reasoning:

1. A circa £2.39 UK buyer-visible price sits inside the live entry-price cluster and below the £3.51 observed median.
2. It is low enough to reduce first-purchase friction without dropping into the very cheapest £0.93 to £1.00 position.
3. The product has six connected tabs and a dashboard, so it has enough substance to avoid competing purely as a £1 commodity.
4. With limited historical shop sales, the near-term commercial priority is sales, trust and review accumulation.
5. The next conversion lever should therefore be **hero-image competitiveness and qualified traffic**, not another price reduction or product rebuild.

## Price guardrail

Do not reduce below £1.99 base solely because cheaper competitors exist. Reconsider only if Etsy traffic reaches the listing but conversion remains weak after hero-image improvements and sufficient qualified visits have accumulated.

Do not increase price until there is evidence of improved conversion/social proof or a materially stronger value presentation.

## Hero-image implications

The first image should communicate, at thumbnail size:

1. this is a polished digital Christmas organisation system;
2. the real dashboard/product is the visual proof;
3. the benefit is calm control of gifts and budget;
4. the Ikhaya aesthetic remains class, elegance, sophistication and organisation.

Avoid generic festive decoration dominating the frame. Christmas cues should be restrained. The product itself should occupy enough of the hero to remain legible on mobile.
